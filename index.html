Why Donki works in Singapore but faced challenges in Malaysia

Experiential retail creates attention. But international success depends on whether product, price, placement, promotion, operations and store economics fit the local market.

01 — Introduction

What is Don Don Donki?

Don Don Donki is a Japanese discount retailer selling groceries, snacks, cosmetics, household products and other imported goods. Its identity is built around Japanese products, dense displays, handwritten-style signs, novelty and a highly experiential shopping environment.

Don Don Donki originated from Japan's Don Quijote chain and expanded overseas under Pan Pacific International Holdings (PPIH). Singapore became its first Southeast Asian market in 2017, while Malaysia entered the network in 2021.

The central question is not simply whether Donki's Japanese concept is attractive. It is whether the entire business system can be adapted to different consumer, economic and competitive conditions.

02 — The Donki Experience

Experience creates attention.
It does not guarantee success.

Experiential retail prioritises discovery, sensory stimulation and emotional engagement. Donki's compressed displays, unexpected product combinations, handwritten signs and dense shelves turn shopping into a form of exploration.

The important analytical shift is that experience should be treated as the first link in a chain, rather than the complete explanation for international performance.

CONTEXT FIT

Singapore: Discovery can become entertainment

🏙️
Urban environment

A highly urbanised and internationally exposed market provides many consumers with experience of different retail formats.

🎌
Japan exposure — hypothesis

Greater exposure to Japan may increase familiarity with Japanese products and retail culture. This is a hypothesis, not proof of causation.

📱
Shareability

The unusual store environment is naturally suited to social media, creating awareness beyond traditional advertising.

🔁
Discovery loop

Novel products can encourage customers to return to see what has changed, potentially converting curiosity into repeat visits.

CONTEXT MISMATCH RISK

Malaysia: Experience needs stronger localisation

🛒
Efficiency trade-off

A dense layout creates value only when customers perceive the search process as enjoyable rather than inconvenient.

🕌
Food accessibility

For a Muslim-majority market, the availability and visibility of halal options can materially affect whether the food experience is broadly accessible.

💰
Price context

If imported products feel expensive, the entertainment value of discovery may not compensate for the price difference.

🔁
Curiosity versus habit

Initial curiosity does not automatically become regular grocery behaviour. Repeat-purchase categories are therefore critical.

→

Key insight: Donki's experience is a competitive advantage, but it works best when the rest of the business system makes the experience useful enough to repeat.

04 — Price

Same tags. Different worlds.

Price perception is relative. A product can look inexpensive in a high-income market but expensive in a lower-income market even when the nominal price is identical.

The correct question is therefore not simply "Is Donki cheap?" but "How affordable is a Donki basket relative to local purchasing power and alternatives?"

LOCALISATION QUESTION

Should the message change by market?

Yes, potentially. A market where consumers already understand Japanese novelty may need less explanation of the "Japan" proposition. A market with stronger price competition may need clearer value communication and more locally relevant products.

ILLUSTRATIVE LOGIC — SENSODYNE-STYLE THINKING

If a product has strong demand but importing it creates a cost or regulatory barrier, management can ask whether the same customer need can be served by a locally sourced or locally certified alternative. The point is not to replace Donki's Japanese identity; it is to localise the parts of the assortment where the Japanese supply chain creates unnecessary friction.

12 — Management Recommendations

What should Donki do?

01

Keep the Japanese core

Do not remove the differentiation that makes Donki distinctive. Localise around the core rather than replacing it.

02

Build local assortment layers

Increase locally relevant everyday products where imported goods create unnecessary price or regulatory friction.

03

Design for repeat purchase

Measure the share of the assortment that creates regular visits, not only novelty traffic.

04

Use catchment-first expansion

Open stores only where target customers, spending power, access and repeat potential are strong enough.

05

Segment the value proposition

Promote discovery to experience-led customers and clear savings to value-conscious customers.

06

Require store-level evidence

Use sales, margin, rent, basket size, frequency and contribution before approving additional expansion.

MANAGEMENT RULEEXPAND ONLY WHEN LOCAL FIT + REPEAT DEMAND + STORE ECONOMICS ALIGN.
13 — Conclusion

Not one store model.
A business system.

Donki's Singapore–Malaysia comparison shows why experiential retail should not be treated as a complete international strategy. The treasure-hunt environment creates attention, but attention must be converted into purchases, repeat behaviour and profitable stores.

Singapore appears to provide a stronger combination of conditions for Donki: high purchasing power, international exposure, established interest in Japanese products and strong urban catchments. Malaysia presents a different environment in which price sensitivity, local competition, halal accessibility, product localisation and store-level economics become more important.

The evidence therefore supports a more nuanced conclusion than "Singapore succeeded and Malaysia failed." PPIH's FY6/26 store rationalisation across Asia shows that underperforming locations were being addressed as part of a broader portfolio strategy. Malaysia's store-count reduction is evidence that expansion needs to be selective, but it does not by itself prove that localisation was the sole cause.

CORE THESIS

Donki should not export one identical store model. It should export a business system that can be localised.
